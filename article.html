Dune: Prophecy of Sound

The most tactile sandstorm you will hear. Our resident acoustics critic dives into Villeneuve's latest fever dream.

97% Essential 2h 45m Dir. Denis Villeneuve

Beneath

the bass notes, beneath the whispered prophecy, there's a rhythm that feels like a planet breathing. Villeneuve understands that every decibel is a narrative choice, and here he uses silence like a blade.

The set pieces are less about spectacle and more about texture: sand that ripples like vinyl static, blades that click like shutter speeds. Rebecca Ferguson and Zendaya trade lines like tactical signals, each pause a different kind of threat.

The Bene Gesserit hymns swell into polyphonic dread, and when the ornithopters rise, the audio pans with the confidence of a turntablist. This is sound design as world-building, a statement on how myth survives by being heard, not merely seen.

“I felt the theater become a cathedral — every speaker a stained-glass window humming with prophecy.”

The third act's duel is less about who wins and more about which frequency survives. And by the time the desert quiets, you'll wonder if silence has ever sounded so loud.
